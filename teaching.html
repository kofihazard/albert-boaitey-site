Teaching

I teach courses in agricultural economics, food systems, and environmental policy. My teaching philosophy emphasizes applied problem‑solving, data‑driven reasoning, and connecting economic concepts to real‑world challenges in agriculture and sustainability.

Courses Taught

Applied Agricultural Economics

Undergraduate

Introduces students to economic decision‑making in agricultural production, markets, and policy. Emphasis on empirical tools, case studies, and contemporary issues in food systems.

Food Systems & Sustainability

Undergraduate

Examines the structure, performance, and sustainability challenges of global food systems, including climate impacts, welfare considerations, and consumer behaviour.

Environmental & Resource Economics

Postgraduate

Covers economic frameworks for environmental policy, valuation, externalities, and incentive design, with applications to agriculture and natural resource management.

Quantitative Methods for Agricultural Policy

Postgraduate

Focuses on empirical tools used in policy evaluation, including causal inference, survey design, and applied econometrics.

Graduate Supervision

I supervise MSc and PhD students working on topics related to livestock sustainability, climate mitigation, animal welfare, and food systems policy. I welcome inquiries from prospective students with strong quantitative skills and an interest in interdisciplinary research.

Teaching Philosophy

My approach to teaching emphasizes:

  • Active learning and applied problem‑solving.
  • Integrating empirical evidence into policy discussions.
  • Developing critical thinking and communication skills.
  • Connecting classroom concepts to real‑world agricultural challenges.
